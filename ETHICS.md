# The ORPHÉE Charter

*Regarder sans prendre. To look without taking.*

Orpheus lost Eurydice because he looked back. A technology that looks at the past should learn from him. These six principles apply from today, including to the software simulator and the web simulator ("the Echo").

They are open to amendment: propose one by opening an issue.

---

## I. A simulation is not an observation

Everything ORPHÉE produces today is simulated, and says so.

- Every image exported from the web simulator carries a visible "artistic simulation, not a real observation" label.
- We will never present a synthetic image, sound or video as a historical document.
- If the project ever records a real measurement, the data will be clearly separated from any visualisation made from it.

*Context:*
- In January 2026, memorials including Dachau, Buchenwald and Neuengamme asked platforms to label or remove AI-generated fake Holocaust images.
- The transparency duties of Article 50 of the EU AI Act have applied since 2 August 2026.

## II. Calibrate before you believe

Every reading will declare its calibration, its uncertainties and its assumptions, and will stay revisable.

*Context:*
- In 2008, Scott de Martinville's 1860 phonautogram was played at double speed and heard as a young girl's voice.
- In 2009, the speed was corrected. It is a man, probably Scott himself, singing slowly.
- A badly calibrated reading can change even the identity of the one speaking.

## III. Look, don't bring back

We will not make the dead speak: no avatars, no "griefbots", no recreated voices. Observing the past is not resurrecting it.

*Context:*
- In 2024, Cambridge researchers called for safeguards against "deadbots".
- Since December 2025, New York has required heirs' consent for commercial digital replicas of deceased people.

## IV. The past has a right to privacy

- No observation will target a person.
- Recent layers, where witnesses are still alive, are excluded by default.
- The collective comes before the individual, and the place before the person.

*Precautionary principle:* should the technology ever work, it would be the ultimate surveillance tool.

## V. Publish the failures

- Protocols and success criteria are pre-registered.
- Raw data, analysis code and calibrations are open.
- Null results are published first.

We would rather be cleanly refuted than wrongly believed.

*A cautionary tale we own:* in 1969, Woodbridge claimed that pottery had "recorded" ambient sound. It has never been convincingly reproduced. We cite it so as not to repeat it.

## VI. No one owns the past

The code is MIT-licensed, with no patents and no exclusivity. A technology able to see the past can belong to no one, and the project must outlive its creators.
